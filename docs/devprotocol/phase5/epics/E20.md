## 1. Epic goal

Make suspension and closure into **states with a history, a scope and a way back**, on all three tiers, and
do it without the platform performing its first hard delete.

Before this epic, `disabled` was a bare boolean on `user` and `shopOwner`. It said an account was suspended
and nothing else: not who suspended it, not why, not when. There was no second place to look — no audit
collection, no event log, no admin note on `user` at all — so the information did not exist rather than
being hard to find. Closure was thinner still: `userDel` stamped `deleted`, a TTL index removed the document
thirty days later, and a customer who re-registered at the same address destroyed the closed one on the spot.

Six decisions replace that, and the shape of all six is the same: **nothing is ever destroyed, and every
state says who put the account into it.**

| Decision | What it settles |
|---|---|
| [ADR-041](../../phase3/adr/ADR-041-retention-overwrites-in-place-nothing-is-destroyed.md) | retention overwrites in place; the document is permanent; the TTL index goes |
| [ADR-042](../../phase3/adr/ADR-042-registration-is-a-pending-redis-record.md) | an unconfirmed registration is a Redis key, not a half-built document |
| [ADR-043](../../phase3/adr/ADR-043-pending-registration-carries-csfle-ciphertext.md) | the pending record carries the deterministic ciphertext, so the confirm copies rather than re-encrypts |
| [ADR-044](../../phase3/adr/ADR-044-suspension-names-an-actor-and-a-reason.md) | `disabled` becomes a triple — the flag, `disabledBy`, `disabledReason` |
| [ADR-045](../../phase3/adr/ADR-045-an-inactive-shop-owner-takes-the-storefront-off-air.md) | an inactive owner's companies and items go `published: false`; the owner republishes by hand |
| [ADR-046](../../phase3/adr/ADR-046-the-retention-window-is-an-undo-window.md) | the thirty days are an **undo** window, taken by registering again |

⚠️ **The organising ruling is one sentence, and everything else is downstream of it:** *"if admin suspend an
account, admin must remove the suspension for allow the shopowner to log in again."* Suspension is the
admin's instrument at both ends. That is why `disabledBy` is always an `admin._id` rather than a
polymorphic actor, why a restore leaves `disabled*` untouched, and why four services now carry a lint rule
refusing to write those three fields at all (E20-S13).

⚠️ **The second is the one that reverses a decision taken the same week.** The platform owner asked *"in the
1-30 days window, what the purpose to keep the user data if he register in that window and new document will
be created ?!?!?"* — and the honest answer was *none*. The window protected nobody and the flow inside it
destroyed the data twenty-seven days early. The correction, *"the 30 days windows is for undo too !"*, is
what turned retention from a countdown into a recovery.

## 2. Scope

| In scope | Out of scope | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `disabledBy`, `disabledReason`, `deletedBy`, `scrubbedAt` on `user` and `shopOwner`, in a new migration | The same fields on `admin` | Nobody decided who suspends an admin, and inventing the actor for that write is a decision this epic has no ruling for |
| The 1000-character cap on `disabledReason`, in the Admin tier's input validation | The cap in `$jsonSchema` | The field is randomly encrypted — a `binData` has no length a validator can read. Presence is all the collection can demand, and it demands it: `dependencies: { disabled: ['disabledReason'] }` |
| Dropping `user.deleted_ttl`, in a second new migration | Editing the migration that created it | Migrations are immutable ([`CLAUDE.md`](../../../../CLAUDE.md)). `INDEXES_USER` still lists the index; the suite asserts the end state instead |
| Suspension and closure cascading to `published: false` on every company and item | Un-suspending re-publishing them | The owner's ruling: *"restoring a shopowner, restore only his account"*. A cascade back would republish a shop the owner may have been suspended for |
| `shopOwnerDel` on the ShopOwner tier — the owner closes their own account | An Admin-tier `userDel` | Still absent, and still E19 §6's question 3. An admin closing somebody else's account is not the exercise of a data-subject right |
| `itemsUpdatePublished`, bounded at 500 ids | Paging the owner's item list | The list is not paged, so "take my whole shop down" is one intent. The bound is the call's, and the frontend chunks to it |
| A day-30 sweeper in the Admin resource service | A TTL index, or any job runner | A TTL index removes whole documents, which is the mechanism ADR-041 exists to retire. Nothing else on this platform runs scheduled work |
| The privacy notice stating all of it | An erasure-request flow | None exists, none is decided, and `marketplace-user`'s notice says so rather than describing a queue nobody operates |
| — | Anything on `admin` | The admin collection is outside the retention design entirely, which ADR-044 records as a known loose end rather than a gap this epic closes. ⚠️ **Half of that loose end was tied off the same day** by the ruling in §6 question 2: an admin account is never closed, so there is nothing for the retention design to reach on `admin` and its absence there is deliberate |

What the epic said when it opened, and what closed it:

- ~~`disabled` is a bare boolean and nothing records who set it or why~~ — it is a triple on both
  collections, written as one by `funShopOwnerUpdateStatus` and `funUserUpdateStatus`, and legible on
  `shopOwnerById` and the customers table.
- ~~A suspended shop owner's storefront stays on the public site~~ — suspending or closing an owner
  unpublishes every company and every item under them, in the same transaction as the write.
- ~~`user.deleted_ttl` removes the document at day 30, the platform's only hard delete~~ — dropped in
  `20260829000100-user-retire-deleted-ttl.js`. The sweeper overwrites `login.email`, `login.password` and
  the personal paths and leaves the row standing for ever.
- ~~A registration writes a half-built document that holds the address until somebody clears it~~ — it
  writes nothing. The submission is one Redis key with a three-day TTL, and expiry *is* abandonment.
- ~~Closing an account is irreversible from the moment it is stamped~~ — it is undoable for thirty days by
  registering again at the same address and confirming, which restores the same `_id`.
- ~~A shop owner has no way to close their own account~~ — `shopOwnerDel`, no arguments, on the ShopOwner
  resource service, with a card in the panel that states the four consequences before it is pressed.

⚠️ **One thing did not move and is the finding worth keeping: no tier but `Admin` can write `disabled*`,
and now nothing can accidentally start.** The enforcement is a lint rule in four services rather than a
runtime check, because the runtime enforcement was never missing — the login gate and every refresh read the
flag. What was missing was anything stopping a future resolver from clearing it.

## 4. Stories

Landing order is the story order: the collection before the writers, the writers before the reads, the reads
before the screens, the notice and the anti-story last.

### E20-S01 — The lifecycle paths, and no TTL index anywhere   `built 2026-08-29`

`marketplace-db-setup`, `feat/account-lifecycle-schema`, commit `e0afc6a`.

`20260829000000-account-lifecycle-fields.js` adds `deletedBy`, `disabledBy`, `disabledReason` (encrypted,
`RANDOM`) and `scrubbedAt` to `user` and `shopOwner`, with `dependencies: { disabled: ['disabledReason'] }`.
`20260829000100-user-retire-deleted-ttl.js` drops `deleted_ttl`.

⚠️ **The migration refuses rather than backfills.** `collMod` never re-validates stored documents, so a
database with a suspended account carrying no reason would accept the new validator and fail the next write
to that document. The migration counts those documents and **stops**, because the alternative is writing a
sentence no admin ever wrote into a field an admin is accountable for.

**Acceptance:** both migrations apply to a fresh database and to one built before them; `db.user.getIndexes()`
lists no `deleted_ttl`; a `$set` of `disabled: true` without `disabledReason` is refused by the collection.

**Fallout, in another repo:** `marketplace-dev-public-authorization`'s integration suite seeded suspensions
as a bare flag and stopped being able to (`28b0bed`, branch `chore/disabled-reason-seed`).

### E20-S02 — The admin writes: an actor, a reason, and a cascade   `built 2026-08-29`

`marketplace-dev-admin-authenticated-resource`, commit `2736521`.

`disabled`, `disabledBy` and `disabledReason` move as one on both collections. **The actor comes off
`ctx.state.user._id`, never the wire.** `disabledReason` is the one nullable argument, because graphql-js
cannot express *"required when this other argument is true"* — `validateDisabledReason` carries that
contract, and the 1000-character cap the collection cannot enforce on a `binData`.

ADR-045's cascade lands here: suspending or closing a shop owner unpublishes their companies and every item
under them, in the same transaction as the status write. **Releasing restores nothing** — the owner's ruling.

`funShopOwnerDelete` also answers 404 rather than 500 for an id naming no open account, and its filter
refuses to re-close one already closed, which would have reset the retention clock.

**Acceptance:** a suspension with no reason is refused before it reaches the collection; the reason over 1000
characters is refused with a message naming the limit; a suspended owner's shops are `published: false` in
the same transaction; lifting the suspension leaves them false.

### E20-S03 — The day-30 sweeper   `built 2026-08-29`

`marketplace-dev-admin-authenticated-resource`, commit `123490f`.

An hourly interval inside this service takes a single-key Redis `SET NX PX` lock and overwrites the personal
data of every `shopOwner` and `user` whose closure is older than the window, leaving the document standing.
The placeholders are ADR-041's, not the implementation's: `deleted-${_id}@invalid.local`, a well-formed
bcrypt hash of random bytes, `Deleted User`.

⚠️ **Both filter clauses are `trusted()`-wrapped.** `sanitizeFilter` would otherwise rewrite them into a
permanent no-op that reports success on every run — a sweeper that scrubs nothing and says it did. That is
why the proof is an integration test against real collections rather than an assertion on the filter object.

`disabled` travels with each candidate so `buildAccountScrub` can **overwrite** `disabledReason` rather than
remove it: the validator refuses a suspended document that loses its reason, and a scrub that unset it would
make the document unwritable for ever.

**Acceptance:** a closure at day 29 is untouched and at day 31 is scrubbed; the scrubbed document still
validates; two instances of the service scrub once between them; the address is free for a new registration.

### E20-S04 — The two admin reads   `built 2026-08-29`

`marketplace-dev-admin-authenticated-resource`, commit `c5b30ca`.

`disabledBy` and `disabledReason` were written by S02 and readable nowhere: randomly encrypted, so a shell
against the collection sees `binData`, and this service — which holds the data key — is the only place they
decrypt. `shopOwnerById` gains the pair on its projection and its type; the customers table gains it on the
row, **since there is no customer detail page to put it on instead** and E19 kept it that way deliberately.

Also corrects `GraphQLUserActiveTbl.deleted`, whose description still said nothing writes it.

**Acceptance:** a suspended owner's reason is legible in the admin app and nowhere else; the customers
table renders it under the status of a suspended row.

### E20-S05 — The owner closes their own account   `built 2026-08-29`

`marketplace-dev-authenticated-resource`, commit `0f12f74`.

`shopOwnerDel` **takes no argument**: the account is the one the session authenticated as, so no id from the
wire can name another owner. The close stamps `deleted` and nothing else — **no `deletedBy`, and its absence
is what says the holder closed it themselves** (ADR-044) — and `disabled` is untouched in either direction,
so a self-closure neither hands the way back to an admin nor launders a standing suspension. `waitApprov`
is left alone because BC-03 bans this tier from writing it at all.

Every company and item goes dark in the same transaction (ADR-045), and every session ends after the write
lands, the caller's included.

**Acceptance:** the mutation's argument list is empty; a suspended owner closing their account is still
suspended when it is restored; the caller's own session is gone on the next request.

### E20-S06 — `itemsUpdatePublished`   `built 2026-08-29`

`marketplace-dev-authenticated-resource`, commit `abb652d`.

The owner's item list is not paged, so *take my whole shop down* is one intent and one call rather than one
round trip per card with a half-published shop visible in between. Bounded at 500 ids.

⚠️ **Ownership is all-or-nothing: one foreign id refuses the whole list.** A partial application is
unreportable behind a `Boolean!`, and silently skipping the ids that did not match would turn the mutation
into an existence oracle. The list is de-duplicated **before** the bound and before the guard, because the
guard counts documents against the length of the list and a repeated id would otherwise refuse a call made
entirely of the owner's own items.

**Acceptance:** 500 ids succeed and 501 are refused; one foreign id refuses all of them; a list containing
the same id twice succeeds.

### E20-S07 — Pending registrations live in Redis   `built 2026-08-29`

`marketplace-dev-public-resource`, commit `084ce1b`.

A submitted registration no longer writes MongoDB. It is a Redis record with a three-day TTL carrying the
pre-minted `_id`, the bcrypt hash and the address as **the same deterministic ciphertext the collection
indexes** (ADR-043) — so the confirmation click copies the ciphertext rather than re-encrypting it, and the
uniqueness check before the click is the index's own answer.

Replay-safe: a lost commit acknowledgement is decided outside the session. **Every outcome answers the same
page** — no enumeration oracle, whether the address is free, taken, pending or closed.

**Acceptance:** an abandoned registration leaves nothing in MongoDB and disappears by itself; a crash between
the commit and the `DEL` replays without a second account; every failure renders the check-your-mail page.

### E20-S08 — A closed account comes back at the click   `built 2026-08-29`

`marketplace-dev-public-resource`, commit `f011b1b`.

ADR-046. Inside the window, signing up again at a closed address **restores** the account rather than
replacing it: the confirm clears `deleted` and `deletedBy`, takes the password just chosen, re-raises
`waitApprov` on the seller tier, and leaves `_id`, `registeredAt` and `disabled*` untouched — so the shops
still hang off it and only Admin lifts a suspension.

`buildAccountScrub` loses this caller and keeps the day-30 sweep, which is what stops the two racing.

⚠️ **Replay recovery had to change with it.** The discriminator is now the address holder's credential rather
than the pre-minted `_id`: a restore mints no id, so an `_id` check answers *no* over a restore that
committed, and the person would be shown a failure over their own recovered account.

**Acceptance:** a confirmed re-registration on day 3 returns the same `_id` with its companies attached, all
`published: false`, `waitApprov` raised, and a standing suspension intact; on day 31 it creates a new account.

### E20-S09 — The admin's suspension form   `built 2026-08-29`

`marketplace-admin`, `feat/suspension-reason`, commit `2df3d35`.

The customers table's Suspend button opens a panel with a mandatory textarea, a 1000-character counter and
the sign-out warning, **replacing a `window.confirm`**. The stored reason prints under the status of every
suspended row. The shop-owner detail page gains a "Suspension reason" row beside the Disabled tick on both
cards, plus a read-only "Suspended by".

⚠️ **The reason travels only with a raised flag — lifting one sends `null`**, which is what the service
`$unset`s. Sending the old reason back alongside `disabled: false` would leave a sanction's text on an
account no longer under one.

**Acceptance:** the tick alone cannot be submitted; the counter refuses the 1001st character; lifting a
suspension sends `null`.

### E20-S10 — The owner's two controls   `built 2026-08-29`

`marketplace-shopowner`, `feat/account-lifecycle-owner`, commit `c937c9e`.

Two things the owner needs once S05 and S06 exist. The catalogue page gains a select-all bar writing the
whole selection through `itemsUpdatePublished`, chunked at 500 — which is how an owner puts back the shop
ADR-045 took down. A fourth sidebar section, `/account`, carries the close-account card.

⚠️ **The card is the only screen on the platform that states the four consequences, and it has to be**: a
refused login is a generic unauthorized on every tier so that an address cannot be probed, which buys silence
at the login screen and has to be paid for in plain words somewhere. It says the shops go dark at once, that
every session ends, how the thirty-day undo is taken — *registering again*, never *logging in* — and that a
suspension survives the whole round trip.

**Acceptance:** the mutation is sent with an empty variable set; the button is dead until the box is ticked;
the copy names the undo as a re-registration.

### E20-S11 — The privacy notice states the account lifetime   `built 2026-08-29`

`marketplace-user`, `feat/privacy-account-retention`, commit `8e6f5ac`.

`/privacy` covered the two nginx log files and stopped there. It now carries three more sections: an
unconfirmed registration expiring by itself after three days and writing nothing; a closed account marked
rather than removed; the thirty days as an undo, ending in an overwrite in place.

⚠️ **It states the weakening, not only the protection.** ADR-046 turned *"your data is gone when you close"*
into *"your data is gone thirty days after you close, and anybody who can read mail at your address can have
it back until then"*. A notice that quietly kept the older, stronger sentence would be describing a platform
this one is not. It also says plainly that there is **no** erasure-request queue, because there is not one.

**Acceptance:** every sentence is asserted byte-for-byte against the code that implements it; the page stays
indexable; the log-file list is still exactly two items.

### E20-S12 — True up what the earlier decisions said   `built 2026-08-29`

Two repositories.

`marketplace-dev-user-authenticated-resource` (`9e92913`): `funUserDel`'s docstring described ADR-011's
Amendment — a TTL index removing the document at day 30, and re-registration destroying it sooner — both
superseded the same week they were written.

This epic file's siblings: [`E19.md`](./E19.md) §6 carried two stale premises, and both are corrected in
place rather than deleted. Question 1 said `user` has no field for a suspension reason; it has one.
Question 3's answer described the TTL purge and the destroy-on-re-registration behaviour; ADR-041 and ADR-046
reversed both.

**Acceptance:** no document under `docs/` or in a service docstring still describes a TTL removal or a
destructive re-registration.

### E20-S13 — Anti-story: no tier but `Admin` writes `disabled*`   `not built` (deliberately — an anti-story)

Four services: `marketplace-dev-authenticated-resource` (`54f3029`),
`marketplace-dev-authenticated-authorization` (`b309808`),
`marketplace-dev-user-authenticated-resource` (`cbf9647`),
`marketplace-dev-user-authenticated-authorization` (`8ee3922`).

The story is that **nothing is built**, and a lint rule in each service keeps it that way. A service on the
ShopOwner or User tier able to write `disabled`, `disabledBy` or `disabledReason` could clear a sanction
standing against the very account making the request.

⚠️ **A write ban, not a ban.** The reads are the enforcement — the login gate and every refresh read the flag
— so four *write* shapes are refused and no read shape is: the object-literal key a `$set` is built from, the
same key quoted, and both assignment forms. Scoped to `src/**`, because the integration suites have to seed a
suspension to test that it is refused.

**Acceptance:** each rule fires on all four shapes and on none of the reads; the integration suites still
seed suspensions.

### E20-S14 — The customer closes their own account   `built 2026-08-29`

`marketplace-user`, `feat/account-close`, commit `517dead`.

E20-S10's card, on the tier that had the mutation first. `/account/close` is a fourth screen in the private
area, reached by a fourth link in `AccountNav`, and it sends `userDel` with an empty variable set, then signs
out and lands on `/`.

⚠️ **The mutation was built on 2026-08-26 and nothing called it.** `userDel` shipped with E19 §6 question 3's
answer and no application on the platform sent it, so for three days the customer half of this epic was a
resolver, a sweeper and a privacy page describing an act the customer could not perform. This story is the
missing surface and no new decision: the four consequences it states are ADR-041's overwrite, ADR-044's
untouched `disabled*` trio, ADR-046's undo and the session teardown, worded to agree with `/privacy` line for
line, because the two pages saying different things about the same day 30 is the failure worth designing
against.

⚠️ **A screen of its own, not a card under the password form** — the one screen in the private area that ends
the session it is standing in, so nothing above it survives the press. And ⚠️ **the one write on this tier
sent with `CTX_USER_RESOURCE` rather than `CTX_ACCOUNT_WRITE`**: invalidating `GraphQLUserMe` would send
`AccountGate` to re-read an account that no longer answers, racing the sign-out for a 401 nobody reads.

**Acceptance:** the variable set is `{}`; the button is dead until the box is ticked and dead again when it is
unticked; the copy names the undo as a re-registration and day 30 as an overwrite; no second `Me` is sent
after the close.

## 5. Dependencies

- **E19** gave the admin the customers table and `userUpdateStatus`. This epic writes two more fields
  through that same mutation and renders them in that same table; without E19 there is no surface to put
  them on.
- **ADR-011** is the soft-delete pattern all of this rests on, and its Amendment of 2026-08-26 is the part
  ADR-041 supersedes.
- **ADR-029** bounds what the admin table may query. Nothing here changed a field's algorithm:
  `disabledReason` is `RANDOM`, which is why it is displayed and never filtered on.
- **ADR-031** — twelve repositories carry a commit, so landing this was twelve commits plus a thirteenth in
  the parent bumping the submodule pointers, made in `d403e3e` once the owner merged the twelve. E20-S14 is
  a fourteenth commit in a repository already on that list, and its pointer waits on its own merge.
- **ADR-038** — commerce is permanently out of scope, which is what removes the usual reason to retain a
  closed account: no disputes, no chargebacks, no refunds. ADR-046's window exists for the person, not for
  the ledger.
